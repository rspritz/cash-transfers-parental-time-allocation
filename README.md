# cash-transfers-parental-time-allocation
In 2021, in response to the COVID-19 pandemic, the child tax credit in the US was extended as part of the American Rescue Plan. Using 2015 to 2022 data from the American Time Use Survey (ATUS) linked to the Current Population Survey (CPS), I estimate a difference-in- differences model to identify the impact the extension had on parental childcare time. I find that the policy extension reduced parental childcare time, consistent with households substituting for formal or paid childcare. However, the extension did not reduce the gap in domestic labor between mothers and fathers that was exacerbated during the pandemic. These results are robust to several specifications and sub-samples. My findings contribute to understanding how unconditional cash transfers affect household behavior and the gender heterogeneity in policy impact. [Read the full paper (PDF)](paper.pdf)
